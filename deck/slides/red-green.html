No. 14

The single most effective practice for a small, slow model is red-green testing, written into its instructions. Write a test that fails. Watch it fail, and check it fails for the right reason. Make the smallest change. Run the whole suite, not just the one test. Then quote the command and its output before claiming anything, and commit. Why does this matter more locally than with Claude Code? Because of the clock. On my setup a bug fix takes a minute or two and a feature three or four, and longer tasks much more. You are not going to sit and watch that, so while you are away the tests are the only reviewer, and the model's own claim that it works is not evidence. I put this in two places: a line in AGENTS.md, and the test-driven-development and verification skills from the superpowers collection. On a real bug fix the model loaded both by itself and ran the full suite before reporting back. It made the run slower, by about a minute, and I think that minute is well spent when nobody is watching.
