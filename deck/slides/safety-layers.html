No. 09

Claude Code gives you a lot of safety for free, so the question is how much of it you can rebuild around pi. Pi's own security page is blunt: there is no sandbox, and every extension runs with your full permissions. I rebuilt it in four layers. A boundary: pi-sandbox, which keeps the agent inside the project and on a short list of domains; Claude Code's equivalent is its built-in bash sandbox. Guards: two small extensions that confirm rm -rf and sudo and refuse writes to .env and .git, which stand in for permission prompts, deny rules and protected paths. Undo: a clone and a branch per task, plus a git stash every turn, which does the job of rewind and also covers what bash changed. Credentials: a local model has no key to leak and no bill. What I have not rebuilt: auto mode's classifier that reviews each action before it runs, and plan mode's hard lock on edits. I use a plan template and approve plans by hand instead.
