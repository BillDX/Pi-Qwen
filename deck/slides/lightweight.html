Why keep it small? Because on a local model every token of standing prompt is time. A Berkeley study called HarnessTax measured what each harness sends before your task: pi about two thousand tokens, Claude Code about twenty-seven thousand with twenty-three tools. On a frontier API that is cheap and fast. On my laptop, which reads prompts at roughly ninety tokens a second, it is twenty seconds against about five minutes every time the cache is cold: a new session, a compaction, a model switch. So I treat the always-on prompt like a budget. Pi gives you four places to put instructions and they cost very different amounts. AGENTS.md is sent in full every request, so mine is about three hundred tokens. Skills cost about a hundred tokens each just to be listed, and their body only loads when used. Prompt templates cost nothing until you type them, which makes them the right home for most of your saved prompts. And avoid SYSTEM.md: it replaces pi's prompt and drops its tool list, not just the opening line.
